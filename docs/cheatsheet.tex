``EdgeBot is a voice-commanded warehouse robot, built fully in simulation. You say \emph{move the red box from
shelf A to the conveyor}. Whisper turns speech to text, a small BERT model finds the task and the details, A* plans

\hd{1. The 4 main questions}
\textbf{What is the project?} Voice $\to$ Whisper $\to$ BERT (intent + slots) $\to$ A* route $\to$ \textbf{PPO
drives} $\to$ \textbf{Shield checks} $\to$ arm picks/places $\to$ spoken reply.\\
\textbf{What problem?} Warehouse layouts change daily, so hand-coded driving fails. The robot \emph{learns} to
drive. Learned policies still crash sometimes, more after 8-bit compression for edge chips. The shield stops that.\\
\textbf{Which RL algorithm?} \textbf{PPO} is deployed. DQN is the baseline. Q-learning/SARSA are the foundation.\\
\textbf{Which papers?} See section 5: 2 classics, 2 safety, 2 compression, 2 recent.

\hd{2. Why software only (no hardware)}
\begin{itemize}
\item RL learns by crashing: PPO needed \textbf{5 million steps}. That's 25 min in simulation vs weeks and broken parts on a real robot.
\item Fair test: every method gets the \textbf{same 300 unseen warehouses}.
\item The course is about algorithms. The simulator is still realistic: Gymnasium API, JetBot size (12 cm), 24-beam lidar, unicycle physics.
\item Exported to ONNX/INT8, the format a Jetson runs. \emph{Limit:} not tested on a real Jetson.
\end{itemize}

\hd{3. The MDP (draw on board)}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lX@{}}
\textbf{State} & 29 numbers: 24 lidar distances + goal distance + $\sin/\cos$ bearing + last action \\
\textbf{Action} & speed $v\in[0,0.5]$ m/s, turn $\omega\in[-2,2]$ rad/s \\
\textbf{Transition} & $x{+}{=}v\cos\theta\,\Delta t,\ y{+}{=}v\sin\theta\,\Delta t,\ \theta{+}{=}\omega\Delta t$, $\Delta t=0.1$ s\\
\textbf{Reward} & $+3\times$progress, $-0.01$/step, small near-obstacle and jerk penalties, \textbf{+10 goal, $-$10 crash}\\
\textbf{$\gamma$} & 0.99
\end{tabularx}

\hd{4. Algorithms: ``Q $\to$ D $\to$ P $\to$ S''}
\textbf{Q --- Q-learning} (tabular TD, off-policy)
\[Q(s,a)\leftarrow Q(s,a)+\alpha\,[\,r+\gamma\max_{a'}Q(s',a')-Q(s,a)\,]\]
\A{Say: ``The bracket is the TD error: what I got plus what I expect next, minus what I predicted.''}
SARSA uses $Q(s',a')$ of the action \emph{actually taken} (on-policy). Result: Q-learning finds the shortest
route ($-11$, optimal); SARSA takes the safer route (Sutton \& Barto cliff walk). \textit{Problem: a table can't
hold continuous lidar} $\to$

\textbf{D --- DQN} (deep, value-based)
\[L=\big(r+\gamma\,Q_{\text{tgt}}(s',\arg\max_{a'}Q(s',a'))-Q(s,a)\big)^2\]
\textbf{Replay buffer} (random old samples, breaks correlation), \textbf{target net} (stable target),
\textbf{Double DQN} (online net picks $a'$, target net scores it, which stops over-estimation), \textbf{Dueling}
($Q=V+A$). 5 discrete moves. 86\% success. \textit{Problem: only discrete moves} $\to$

\textbf{P --- PPO} (actor-critic, \textbf{deployed})
\[L^{\text{CLIP}}=\mathbb{E}\big[\min(\rho\hat A,\ \text{clip}(\rho,0.8,1.2)\hat A)\big],\quad \rho=\tfrac{\pi_{\text{new}}(a|s)}{\pi_{\text{old}}(a|s)}\]
Actor outputs a Gaussian over $(v,\omega)$; the critic gives $V(s)$; advantage $\hat A$ from GAE ($\lambda=0.95$).
\A{Say: ``The clip stops any single update from changing an action's probability by more than 20\%. That keeps
training stable.''}
16 parallel envs, 5M steps, \textbf{96\% success}, actor is 26 KB in INT8. \textit{Problem: still crashes 2--3\%} $\to$

\textbf{S --- Shield} (my novelty, \texttt{edgebot/rl/shield.py})
\begin{enumerate}
\item The policy proposes $(v,\omega)$.
\item The lidar scan becomes obstacle points.
\item Simulate 5 steps (0.5 s) ahead with the unicycle equations.
\item Too close (radius + 4 cm)? Swap in the nearest safe move. A spin is always safe.
\end{enumerate}

\hd{6. Likely questions}
\Q{Is the shield really new?}
\A{Shields exist since 2018. What's new is the combination: map-free, training-free, protecting 8-bit edge
policies, tested FP32 vs INT8. \emph{To the best of my knowledge}, not done before.}
\Q{On-policy vs off-policy?}
\A{On-policy (SARSA, PPO) learns from its own current behaviour. Off-policy (Q-learning, DQN) can reuse old data from a replay buffer.}
\Q{Why PPO over DQN?}
\A{Continuous speed and steering, smoother, more successful (96\% vs 86\%), and stable to train.}
\Q{Why a critic in PPO?}
\A{To compute the advantage. Subtracting the baseline $V(s)$ lowers gradient variance.}
\Q{What is GAE $\lambda$?}
\A{$\lambda=0$: one-step TD (low variance, biased). $\lambda=1$: Monte Carlo (unbiased, noisy). I used 0.95.}
\Q{Exploration?}
\A{DQN: $\varepsilon$-greedy, decaying. PPO: samples from its Gaussian, and the learned $\sigma$ shrinks over training.}
\Q{Why reward progress, not just +10?}
\A{A sparse reward almost never fires by chance. Rewarding progress gives a learning signal on every step (reward shaping).}
\Q{Bellman optimality?}
\A{$Q^*(s,a)=\mathbb{E}[r+\gamma\max_{a'}Q^*(s',a')]$. Q-learning is a sampled version of it.}
\Q{Why did INT8 hurt the pixel DQN?}
\A{Dynamic per-tensor scaling crushes small CNN values and flips which action is max. Calibrated static INT8 recovers it (47\%$\to$74\%).}
\Q{Can the shield fail?}
\A{Yes: thin poles between lidar beams, moving obstacles, and the 0.5 s horizon. Future work: a denser lidar and motion prediction.}
\Q{How was evaluation fair?}
\A{Test seeds start at 1,000,000, so they never overlap training. The same 300 layouts are used for every method.}
\Q{Sim-to-real?}
\A{Domain randomisation (noise, friction), then TensorRT on a Jetson. The ONNX files are ready.}
